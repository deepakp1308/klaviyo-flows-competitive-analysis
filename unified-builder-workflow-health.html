Customer Targeting — Executive Priority Ranking

Who to prioritize for FY27 Builder ARR · Full population BigQuery analysis (1,651,592 eligible customers) · KMeans clustering on 21 Builder behavioral features · Roadmap executive summary →

Bottom line: Close the $920K gap to $21M by executing Bundle A in Q1–Q2 (Universal Content + migration + Code Mode), running Bundle B in parallel (rendering/trust for high-friction cohorts), and deferring dormant-free activation until foundation ships. 903,111 customers (55% of cohort) in the top 6 clusters deliver $16.67M (83% of FY27 EV). The Builder opportunity is concentrated in high-completion, high-MRR senders — not the dormant free base.

Executive Key Takeaways

  1. Win the $21M on 47% of customers, not the whole base. 780,441 Bundle A customers account for $16.67M (83%) of FY27 EV. C7 alone is 21% of cohort but $4K EV. Do not spread Q1 capacity across dormant free users.
  2. Run two parallel tracks: expand power users, fix frustrated senders. Bundle A (Q1–Q2): Universal Content + Code Mode for C2/C5/C6. Bundle B (parallel Q1): rendering/trust for C8/C9 before any activation push.
  3. Completion beats creation; activity beats list size. email_completion_rate (+0.18) and builder_active_days_90d (+0.35) drive revenue; friction_score (−0.23) and create_no_publish_rate (−0.21) drag it down. Invest in publish completion and trust, not bigger lists.
  4. Universal Content dominates every Wave 1 cluster. C2, C5, C3, C6, C1, and C11 all rank Universal Content #1 or #2. Any Q1 slip on UC primitive + migration directly hits the revenue critical path.
  5. Defer dormant-base activation to Q2–Q3. Bundle C (566,203 customers) contributes $2.65M EV. Templates and AI Setup Agent matter — but only after Bundle B removes the friction blocking customers who already show intent.
  6. Sequencing is non-negotiable. Q1: foundation + UC primitive + migration starts. Q2: migration ships + Code Mode + templates. Q3: goal-driven agent + omni-channel. Reshuffling to chase C7 volume adds reach, not revenue.

Analysis 1 — Three synthesis insights (Catalog & lifecycle)

  1. The catalog is broad but shallow on lifecycle intent. 709 gallery templates skew seasonal (148), newsletter (96), and vertical décor (Restaurant, Music, Sports) — not goal-filtered lifecycle kits (welcome, abandoned cart, win-back) the way Klaviyo surfaces 160+ templates by use case.
  2. NUNI coverage is partial in BQ. Only 118/709 gallery rows are NUNI-capable; the runtime NUNI JSON catalog (~260+) is the authoritative New Builder set. SMS/WhatsApp do not appear as first-class gallery templates — email (+ RSS-to-email, Eventbrite) only.
  3. AutoConnect templates are integration-specific, not vertical lifecycle. 22 templates (Eventbrite, Facebook Events, SurveyGizmo) solve narrow integration sends; they do not substitute for ecommerce lifecycle or B2B welcome series customers request in VoC.

Executive Summary — What You Should Know

  1. 709 production gallery templates exist, but only 3.3% of paid customers used NUNI CA templates in the last year. 79% still use classic paid gallery layouts. The New Builder template investment is not yet the default paid experience.

  3. The catalog is seasonally deep, lifecycle shallow. 148 holiday templates vs zero goal-filtered lifecycle series (welcome, abandoned cart/checkout, win-back) that Klaviyo ships publicly.
  4. VoC + unitQ (+167% template dissatisfaction) asks for goal-based, vertical lifecycle kits and wizard discoverability — not more decorative categories. Half the pain is portability (UC) and NUNI/Classic bifurcation.
  5. Closing the top 5 gaps maps to ~55–60% of existing Bundle A/C cluster EV (C2, C4, C5, C12). Template work is on the revenue critical path only when sequenced after Q1 trust + UC primitive.
